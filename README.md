This is a continuously updated **index of Chinese historical visual resources**. We have selected worthwhile resources and entry points from universities, libraries, museums, news agencies, media outlets, non-profit projects, and photographers' websites around the world. These entries come with English guides, categorizations, and specific examples to help you navigate to the original sites for research and reference.

From late Qing street scenes to the Reform and Opening-up, from county towns in the 1990s to the everyday life of a decade ago—find visual records of a changing China.


Writing fiction, making games, shooting films, seeking art references, or simply wanting to see the China of the past—start here.

**How it is maintained.** The project is maintained by media professionals with two to three decades of experience in content production and editorial work. AI-assisted deep research helps broaden the search; people make the final decisions, including selection, deduplication, description, and checks against original sources where possible.

**Two complementary versions.** GitHub provides the human-readable guide, editorial context, and source notes. A [structured bilingual dataset on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/BreadStudio/china-historical-visual-resources-index) makes the same index easier to search, reuse, and build upon programmatically.

### Historical Photographs of China

[Image Archive](https://hpcbristol.net/) · [Example: Hawker outside basket shop, Beijing](https://hpcbristol.net/visual/Hv28-20)

**Living details of old China.** An entry to historical photography maintained by the University of Bristol, ideal for finding figures, shops, architecture, occupations, and street scenes. The Beijing hawker example above is dated 1933–1946, with details as fine as storefronts and items for sale available for search clues.

<a id="john-thomson"></a>

### MIT Visualizing Cultures | John Thomson's China

[Theme Entry](https://visualizingcultures.mit.edu/john_thomson_china_03/index.html)

**Look at late Qing people and streets through a 19th-century photography collection.** MIT showcases the four-volume *Illustrations of China and Its People* published in 1873–1874, sourced from Yale University. Great for finding references on clothing, occupations, and architecture; note that the publication year differs from the year the photos were taken.

<a id="beijing-silvermine"></a>

### Beijing Silvermine

[Project Introduction](https://www.beijingsilvermine.com/about) · [Curated Gallery](https://www.beijingsilvermine.com/gallery)

**History is also hidden in the film rolls of ordinary people.** An archive of folk negatives collected and organized by Thomas Sauvin, officially spanning 1985–2005. Ideal for finding visual clues about family, gatherings, travel, and consumer life. The online presence is a curated exhibition, not a fully searchable database of all negatives.

<a id="commons"></a>

### Wikimedia Commons | Exploring Images of China by Decade

[Category: China in the 2000s](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:China_in_the_2000s) · [Example: A student having lunch in Tieling High School, 2018](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:A_student_having_lunch_in_Tieling_High_School.jpg)

**Campuses, streets, and daily life from a decade ago also deserve a record.** A community-maintained image repository where you can continue searching by year, location, and theme. The example was taken on May 29, 2018, and its file page indicates CC BY-SA 4.0; other images must be checked individually for date, source, and license.

<a id="national-album"></a>

### Xinhua News Agency | National Album

[Column and Episode Directory](https://www.xinhuanet.com/video/gjxc/index.htm)

**Recover the details of changing lives, from going to the market to self-employed workers and popular colors.** A micro-documentary column based on historical photos from the China Photo Archive. The directory includes themes like "Let's Go to the Market," "I am Self-Employed," and "Popular Colors of That Year." Useful for discovering topics and clues for historical photos. These are edited thematic episodes, not raw photo retrieval databases, and actual video playback has not been verified.

### Harvard | Joseph F. Rock Collection

[Collection Overview and Online Access](https://library.harvard.edu/collections/joseph-f-rock-collection)

**Shift your gaze from coastal cities to the Southwest and Northwest.** Harvard's collection of historical photographs by Joseph F. Rock covers Yunnan, Sichuan, Gansu, and Tibet. Ideal for finding local figures, expedition activities, and natural environments.

### The Tibet Album

[Project Entry](https://tibet.prm.ox.ac.uk/)

**Enter the visual records of Tibet from 1920–1950 by person, place, and album.** A collaborative project between the Pitt Rivers Museum at the University of Oxford and the British Museum, suitable for references on local environments, figures, and historical travel.

### UW–Madison | East Asian Collection

[East Asian Collection](https://search.library.wisc.edu/digital/AEastAsian)

**Keep another search window open for old Chinese photos and postcards.** The East Asian Digital Collection at the University of Wisconsin–Madison allows for continued searches of China-related visual materials; the collection also covers other East Asian regions.

### Taiwan Memory

[Search Entry](https://tm.ncl.edu.tw/index)

**Trace the historical memory of Taiwan from a place and a period of time.** An entry to local history and digital archives where you can continue searching for old photographs, postcards, and other materials, ideal for topics in urban and everyday life history.

<a id="hawaii"></a>

### University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa | Photographs of China, 1987-1988

[Collection Directory](https://digital.library.manoa.hawaii.edu/collections/show/95) · [Example: Hard sleeper carriage, 1988](https://digital.library.manoa.hawaii.edu/items/show/53292)

**Refining the daily life of the 1980s down to a hard sleeper train carriage.** Photographs from two overland trips to China by Michael A. Chopey. The directory includes markets, food, hotels, transportation, and town life in places like Shanghai, Fujian, Guangdong, and Yunnan. The hard sleeper example is dated 1988 and marked CC-BY; other materials should be checked on their respective pages.

### Ian Teh | Undercurrents

[Photography Project and Captions](https://www.ianteh.com/undercurrents-1)

**Turn your attention to border towns and see China at the turn of the century.** The author notes these five sets of stories in China were photographed between 1999–2008. The captions cover residential construction, dining, entertainment, and cross-border life in places like Tumen, Hunchun, Suifenhe, and Dongning, as well as changes in the Three Gorges region. Good for supplementing environmental references outside major cities; the time range applies to the entire project.

<a id="chinafile"></a>

### ChinaFile | Living by the Rivers

[Living by the Rivers](https://www.chinafile.com/multimedia/depth-of-field/living-rivers)

**Follow the rivers to find a cross-section of life in China during the 2010s.** This photography guide published in 2019 connects projects by multiple photographers, including series that began shooting in 2018. Useful for continuing to discover documentary photography of local life and social change; some works require redirecting to the author's website, and the publication year does not equal the shooting year of all photos.

<a id="hku"></a>

### University of Hong Kong | Hong Kong Image Database

[Hong Kong Image Database](https://digitalrepository.lib.hku.hk/hkimage)

**Look at Hong Kong, but also at villages, fisheries, housing, and infrastructure.** The HKU collection description covers the 1840s to the 1990s, with subjects including figures, rural areas, agriculture, fisheries, industry, and urban construction. Suitable for continued searching by location and life themes; the exact dates and access conditions of specific photos are subject to the entries themselves.

### Virtual Shanghai

[Project Overview](https://www.virtualshanghai.net/Presentation/Overview) · [Example: Astor House Hotel towards Suzhou Creek](https://www.virtualshanghai.net/Photos/Images?ID=1)

**Give the architecture in old photos a place on the map.** Shanghai historical photos, maps, and urban research materials, ideal for finding streets, transportation, architecture, and spatial relationships. The Astor House Hotel entry above can serve as a starting point.

### North China Railway Archive

[Search Entry](https://codh.rois.ac.jp/north-china-railway/) · [Open Access Statement (Chinese)](https://codh.rois.ac.jp/north-china-railway/announcement/index.html.zh-cn)

**Follow the railways and stations to find North China cities and landscapes.** Based on the photographic materials collected by Kyoto University, searchable by shooting date, station, and tags. Materials include wartime propaganda photography; some search tags and descriptions are AI-generated and should be distinguished from original records.

### Wellcome | Peking Union Medical College Architectural Archives

[1922 Archives and Access](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/pf63heps)

**Small but specific architectural references: how a hospital was documented.** Historical and architectural archives of the Peking Union Medical College from 1922. The page lists 29 digital images. Suitable for researching medical institution spaces; marked CC BY-NC 4.0.

### MIT | The Empress Dowager and Photography

[Illustrated Essay](https://visualizingcultures.mit.edu/empress_dowager/cx_essay02.html)

**Look at Qing court imagery down to the jewelry, screens, and seating.** MIT's illustrated essay on Empress Dowager Cixi is useful for references on styling and furnishings. These photographic settings must be distinguished from everyday living spaces.

<a id="greg-girard"></a>

### Greg Girard | Shanghai and Hong Kong Photography

[Author Website and Photo Captions](https://www.greggirard.com/) · [Phantom Shanghai](https://www.greggirard.com/phantom-shanghai/)

**The city in memory might be just one demolition away.** The author's website lists photos of Yuyuan Road in 2001, demolition on Fuxing Road in 2002, and street scenes in 2005, ideal for finding old houses, streets, and urban changes in Shanghai in the 2000s. There is also the HK:PM Hong Kong nightlife theme, covering 1974–1989. This is a selected portfolio of the photographer's work.

<a id="shanghai-street-stories"></a>

### Shanghai Street Stories

[Example: A Slice of Time on Moganshan Road](https://shanghaistreetstories.com/?p=8941)

**Urban change often starts with a wall or a small shop.** Photo-essays of neighborhoods are useful for finding references on Shanghai alleyways, residents, and demolition changes. This article on Moganshan Road was published in 2015 and explicitly reviews photos from around 2009; when using it, distinguish between the article date, the revisit time, and the shooting time.

### Edward Burtynsky | China

[China Photography Series](https://www.edwardburtynsky.com/projects/photographs/china)

**Beyond streets and alleys, there are factories and construction sites that shape the city.** The author's China series is organized by themes like manufacturing, old industry, recycling, shipyards, urban renewal, and the Three Gorges. Ideal for finding references on industrial spaces, production environments, and large-scale construction. The page mainly serves as a portfolio showcase, and specific photo dates must be checked in the artwork records.

## Why We Built This List

If you were to recreate a street from the Republican era, besides the architecture, what else would you need to know?

What kind of signs hang in front of the shops, what do the pedestrians wear, what is placed on the side of the street, and what do the stations and shops look like? The details that make a scene truly believable are often these inconspicuous things.

With such creative questions in mind, we started looking for Chinese historical visual materials. AI is great at helping people find clues, but once found, you still have to open the original sites, identify the sources, and distinguish between accessible collections and temporarily unavailable pages.

As the materials accumulated, we felt that rather than leaving these entries scattered in chat logs and bookmarks, it would be better to string them together: add guides, categorize them by use, make it easier to find references later, and share them with those who have the same needs.

Thus, this list was born. We hope that the next time you need to look for the China of the past, you can take a few less detours.
